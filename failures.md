# Failure log

## 001: "Three Rooms" (2026-09-26)

Ryan's verdict: "That was not good. I would consider that a failure."

### Autopsy

1. **Thesis in a sketch costume.** The shape (kills, dies, kills-wrong) is visible in the first ten seconds, so there's no discovery, only illustration. A sketch needs a game that escalates. This one just repeated.
2. **The jokes aren't jokes.** The Cleveland line is filler, the dating line is generic, and everything leans on one casserole bit. It asked the reader to laugh at the idea of comedy instead of writing comedy.
3. **The stage directions do the laughing.** "The place comes unglued" tells you it's funny instead of making it funny. The imitation-of-funny tell.
4. **Marcus has no point of view.** He's a premise delivery device. Anyone could say those lines.
5. **One draft, shipped.** Sketch writing is rewriting. This got none.

### Lesson for the skill

Jokes first, premise second. Character who wants something, one game, escalate until it breaks. Never let a stage direction laugh for you. Rewrite before shipping.

## 002: "We're a Family Here" (2026-09-26)

Ryan's verdict: 4.5/10. "You made a boring sketch." But sincere: the first one wasn't an SNL sketch; this one very well could be. Much better than attempt one, still not great.

### Autopsy

Not broken, boring. The craft fixes worked (Dave wants something, jokes in dialogue, real escalation), but every beat was the expected next step of the premise: calls him Dad, brings casserole, tattles like a sibling, gets the HR talk. The audience knows the whole sketch by beat two. Escalation was linear, never surprising.

### Lesson for the skill

Craft gets you to a real sketch. Surprise gets you to a funny one. The game has to mutate, invert, or break, not just proceed. Next attempt needs a left turn the premise doesn't predict.
